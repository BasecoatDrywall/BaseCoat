# Basecoat Service Terms — Draft for Attorney Review

**This is a working draft, not a ready-to-sign contract or legal advice.** Before use, complete the company name and contact details, service area, license information, insurance disclosures, project-specific terms, and every notice required where the work will be performed. Have a construction attorney review it for each applicable jurisdiction and type of work.

## 1. Scope and written approval

The signed proposal, attached plans, and signed change orders describe the work and materials Basecoat will provide. A customer request or estimate is not an accepted work order. Changes to scope, materials, price, or schedule require written approval before the added work begins, except for emergency measures allowed by law. The signed project documents will identify the agreed price, schedule, and payment milestones.

## 2. Payment

The customer will pay the amounts and milestones stated in the signed project documents. Invoices will identify amounts due and due dates. Deposits, progress payments, payment methods, and any late charges must comply with applicable law and be disclosed in the signed documents. Nothing in these draft terms authorizes an unlawful fee or deduction.

## 3. Overdue amounts and pause of work

If an undisputed amount becomes overdue, Basecoat may give written notice describing the amount due and a reasonable opportunity to cure. If the issue remains unresolved, Basecoat may pause or reschedule work only as the signed contract and applicable law permit. Basecoat may pursue lawful collection, dispute resolution, or a construction lien only when legally available and within all required deadlines and notice rules.

No term authorizes Basecoat or its workers to trespass, enter without lawful access, or damage, dismantle, or destroy installed work or other property because an invoice is unpaid. Uninstalled materials may be retrieved only when the contract and applicable law permit it, Basecoat has lawful access, and retrieval can be done safely. The customer is responsible only for amounts and costs legally recoverable under the signed contract and applicable law.

## 4. Site access, hazards, and responsibility

The customer will provide the agreed site access and disclose known conditions or hazards relevant to the work. Each party remains responsible for its own acts and omissions to the extent required by law. These terms do not excuse Basecoat from responsibility for property damage caused by its negligence or intentional misconduct, and do not waive rights that cannot legally be waived.

## 5. Inspection, completion, and warranty

The project documents should identify inspection steps, substantial completion, punch-list work, and any express warranty. Any inspection report in the Basecoat app is a project record and does not replace an inspection required by a building department, insurer, or other authority. Non-waivable statutory rights remain in force.

## 6. Customer requests and acceptance

Submitting a request through the app asks Basecoat to review the proposed work. It does not create a contract or guarantee a price, start date, or acceptance. Work begins only after Basecoat accepts the request and the parties complete the required written project documents.

## 7. Project information and photographs

Basecoat may use project messages, measurements, plans, and photographs to estimate, schedule, perform, document, and resolve questions about the requested work. Obtain the customer's separate permission before using identifiable project photographs or details in public advertising or promotional materials.

## 8. Required notices and applicable law

The final project contract must include any required written scope, contractor contact and license details, estimated dates, payment schedule, cancellation notices, lien notices, escrow or bond disclosures, insurance disclosures, permits, and local consumer protections. Mandatory law at the job site controls over any inconsistent contract language. The parties should identify any required dispute process and governing law in the final signed documents.

## Location-specific review reminder

This draft is not a Michigan-approved contract. Michigan's Attorney General advises homeowners to use a signed written contract with the work, payment terms, dates, and contractor information. Cancellation rights depend on the transaction; Michigan identifies different periods for certain in-home solicitations and contracts financed over time. Review the [Michigan Attorney General's Building and Remodeling Advice](https://www.michigan.gov/consumerprotection/protect-yourself/consumer-alerts/home-health/building-and-remodeling-advice-for-homeowners), [Michigan Contract Cancellation Information](https://www.michigan.gov/consumerprotection/protect-yourself/consumer-alerts/shopping/contract-cancellation-information-for-consumers), and applicable Michigan statutes and local licensing requirements. Add the correct project-specific notices before using any customer contract.
